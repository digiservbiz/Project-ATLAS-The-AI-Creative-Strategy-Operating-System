# ATLAS — Product Vision

## Vision statement

ATLAS will be an AI operating system for creative strategy: a persistent, research-driven environment where specialized AI agents collaborate with humans to turn business inputs and evidence into testable marketing strategies and creative assets.

## Product promise

ATLAS should help a team move from **"We have a product"** to **"We have a researched, prioritized, testable creative strategy"** without losing the reasoning, evidence, or learnings along the way.

## User experience principles

### 1. Start from the business problem

The user should not need to know which agent to invoke. They describe the goal, product, market, constraints, and available evidence; the Orchestrator determines the required workflow.

### 2. Show the work product, not hidden chain-of-thought

ATLAS should expose useful artifacts such as evidence, assumptions, decisions, outputs, confidence, and validation results. It must not expose private chain-of-thought or internal hidden reasoning.

### 3. Keep strategy connected

A campaign should have a coherent chain:

`Customer insight → problem/desire → positioning → offer → angle → hook → creative → landing-page message → test → result → learning`

### 4. Make alternatives explicit

When uncertainty is high, ATLAS should produce ranked hypotheses or alternatives rather than pretending there is one objectively correct creative direction.

### 5. Preserve human ownership

Users can edit, approve, reject, lock, or override important outputs. The system records those decisions for future learning.

## Product pillars

### Research intelligence

Collect and synthesize market, customer, product, competitor, review, and trend evidence.

### Strategic intelligence

Convert evidence into positioning, offers, messages, angles, and testing hypotheses.

### Creative intelligence

Turn strategy into hooks, scripts, creative briefs, UGC concepts, shot plans, and variants.

### Conversion intelligence

Maintain message continuity between advertising, offers, landing pages, and conversion flows.

### Performance intelligence

Interpret results and convert them into explicit learnings and next tests.

### Organizational memory

Remember brand rules, approved positioning, prior experiments, winning/losing patterns, and reusable knowledge without confusing brand-specific memory with general marketing knowledge.

## First ideal workflow

A user creates a campaign and provides:

- Brand
- Product
- Market/country
- Target audience
- Price/offer
- Existing assets
- Constraints
- Optional competitors
- Optional performance data

ATLAS then:

1. Validates the brief.
2. Identifies missing information.
3. Builds a research plan.
4. Collects/synthesizes evidence.
5. Creates customer and product insights.
6. Develops positioning and offer hypotheses.
7. Generates and clusters creative angles.
8. Prioritizes hooks.
9. Produces scripts/creative briefs.
10. Checks creative-to-landing-page message continuity.
11. Creates a testing matrix.
12. Presents the package for human approval.
13. Stores approved strategy and test hypotheses in campaign memory.

## Long-term product direction

The platform should evolve from a creative strategy workspace into a broader marketing operating system while keeping a strong boundary between:

- **General knowledge** — reusable marketing principles and reference material.
- **Brand knowledge** — facts, voice, positioning, constraints, and approved claims.
- **Campaign memory** — experiments, outputs, decisions, and performance.
- **Execution state** — current workflow runs and tool actions.

## Product boundaries

ATLAS is a decision-support and content-generation system. It should not claim that a generated creative will definitely win, invent customer evidence, fabricate performance data, or silently take consequential external actions.

## North-star outcome

The north-star outcome is not the number of generated assets. It is the number of **useful, evidence-grounded creative hypotheses that become validated campaign learnings**.
